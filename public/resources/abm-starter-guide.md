# Your ABM starter study guide

An original introductory learning resource. Use alongside your teacher's materials; it is not an official DepEd module or financial advice.

## 1. Start with the business question

A business creates value by solving a problem for a customer. Before planning a product, ask:

- Who has this problem?
- What do they do about it now?
- Why would they choose your solution?
- What will it cost to provide that solution responsibly?

Try it: choose a school canteen product. Describe its customers, the need it meets, and one possible improvement. Do not collect personal data without permission.

## 2. Understand the accounting equation

Assets = Liabilities + Owner's Equity

- Assets are resources controlled by the business, such as cash or equipment.
- Liabilities are obligations owed to others.
- Owner's equity is the residual interest in the assets after deducting liabilities.

Example: an owner contributes PHP 5,000 cash. Assets increase by PHP 5,000 and owner's equity increases by PHP 5,000. If the business later borrows PHP 2,000, cash and liabilities both increase by PHP 2,000. The equation remains balanced.

Check yourself: a business has PHP 10,000 in assets and PHP 3,000 in liabilities. Its owner's equity is PHP 7,000.

## 3. Tell markup and margin apart

If a product costs PHP 60 and sells for PHP 100:

- Gross profit per unit = Selling price - Cost = PHP 40.
- Markup on cost = Gross profit / Cost x 100 = 66.67%.
- Gross margin = Gross profit / Selling price x 100 = 40%.

These figures do not automatically represent net profit. Rent, wages, transport, spoilage, and other operating expenses may also need to be covered.

Try it: calculate markup and margin for a product costing PHP 80 and selling for PHP 120. Answers: markup 50%; gross margin approximately 33.33%.

## 4. Build a simple marketing plan

1. Identify one customer group and its specific need.
2. Research respectfully: ask permission and avoid gathering unnecessary personal information.
3. Describe your product and the benefit it provides.
4. Set a price using costs, customer research, and alternatives.
5. Choose a realistic place or channel to reach customers.
6. Plan a truthful promotion with a clear message.
7. Decide what evidence would show whether the idea works.

## 5. Keep responsible business decisions visible

Ask who may benefit or be harmed by a decision. Check product safety, honest claims, fair treatment, privacy, accessibility, and environmental impact. Follow school approval procedures before any selling activity.

## 6. Practice a weekly study routine

- Review one new concept and explain it in your own words.
- Work through two practice problems without looking at the answers.
- Correct mistakes and write down why they occurred.
- Connect the topic to a real business or classroom project.
- Bring one specific question to your next class.
